# Custom Branded Shirts — What Else Needs to Be in Place

Before ordering, decide **why** you're making them — the answer changes almost everything below.

| Purpose | Changes |
|---|---|
| **Staff/team uniforms** | Optimize for durability + consistent sizing, not sales. Skip most of the "selling it" section. |
| **Promo giveaways** (events, order bundles, referral rewards) | Optimize for low cost per unit and fast turnaround, not premium fabric. |
| **A sellable product line on the platform itself** | Treat it like launching any other product — full checklist below applies. |

Pick one (or note if it's a mix) before you order anything — it decides the fabric, the MOQ, and the budget.

---

## 1. Brand basics (lock these down first — everything else depends on them)

- [ ] **Final logo file** — vector (SVG/AI/EPS), not a screenshot or low-res PNG. A print shop cannot make a clean shirt from a blurry logo.
- [ ] **Approved color values** — the exact orange/black/white hex codes already used across the storefront (`#EA580C`-range orange is the brand's current CTA color — confirm the exact hex before printing, don't eyeball it from a screen).
- [ ] **Logo usage rules** — minimum size, clear space around it, and which logo variant (full lockup vs. icon-only) goes on a small shirt chest print vs. a large back print.
- [ ] **One-line tagline or slogan**, if you want text on the shirt beyond the logo (e.g. something short enough to read from a distance — not a paragraph).

If any of this doesn't exist as an approved, final asset yet, get that signed off *before* getting quotes — reprinting because the logo was wrong is the single most common wasted cost in merch runs.

---

## 2. Production decisions

- [ ] **Local Nigerian printer vs. bulk from China.** Local = faster turnaround, easier to fix mistakes, better for small runs. China-sourced bulk = cheaper per unit at volume, but you're back to the same shipping-time tradeoff the whole platform exists to solve for your buyers — a little ironic if the answer is "wait 4 weeks" for your own merch. For a first run, go local.
- [ ] **Printing method** — screen printing (best for large flat-color runs, cheaper at volume), DTF/DTG (better for detailed multi-color logos, fine for smaller runs), or embroidery (most durable and premium-looking, best for a small chest logo, more expensive per unit).
- [ ] **Fabric** — 100% cotton (breathable, higher-end feel, shrinks slightly) vs. cotton-polyester blend (cheaper, more durable for frequent wear, better for uniforms).
- [ ] **Get 2–3 quotes** from different printers before committing — price and quality both vary a lot at this stage in Nigeria.
- [ ] **Order one physical sample/prototype first.** Never go straight to a bulk order. Check the print doesn't crack or fade after a real wash, and that the fabric weight feels right in person.

---

## 3. Sizing & inventory

- [ ] **A real size chart** (S/M/L/XL/XXL with actual chest/length measurements) — don't guess, and don't assume unisex sizing fits everyone comfortably.
- [ ] **Decide the size/color spread for the first order** (e.g. more M/L than XS/XXL, based on who's actually wearing them) rather than ordering an even split across every size.
- [ ] If this becomes a sellable product: the platform already supports **size/color as product variants** (the same option system vendors use for packaging/color today) — plan the size+color combinations as variants from day one rather than bolting it on later.

---

## 4. If you're selling these (not just giving them away)

- [ ] **Product photography** — on a person/mannequin, not flat on a table, so buyers can judge fit.
- [ ] **A written size guide** on the product page — the #1 return reason for apparel anywhere is "wrong size."
- [ ] **A clear apparel return/exchange policy**, separate from your general return policy — printed/branded items are often exchange-only (not full refund) once worn, for hygiene reasons; decide this explicitly and state it, don't leave it ambiguous.
- [ ] **Landed cost → margin → retail price math**, done properly: unit cost + printing cost + packaging + your commission/overhead, before you pick a price.
- [ ] **Business registration/invoicing for the merch line**, if it's a distinct revenue stream you'll want clean books for later.

---

## 5. Marketing the launch

- [ ] Decide if it's a **limited/seasonal drop** (creates urgency) or a permanent catalog item.
- [ ] Consider bundling it — "free shirt with orders over ₦X" or as a loyalty/referral reward — rather than only selling it standalone.
- [ ] Get staff actually wearing them at any in-person events/deliveries — the cheapest brand marketing you'll ever do is a shirt someone else sees in public.

---

## The one thing to not skip

Whatever else gets cut for budget or time, **do not skip the physical sample step**. A logo that looks perfect on screen can print blurry, the wrong shade of orange, or crack after one wash — and you only find out for certain once you've held the actual shirt.
