## Breaking down the three big objections

### "Alibaba is cheaper because I'm buying direct from China."
You're buying direct from a *stranger* in China, with a 30–45 day wait and zero real leverage if the shipment doesn't match what you ordered — Alibaba's dispute system is built for B2B bulk buyers, not someone ordering a few units for themselves or resale. ChinaInNigeria vendors already imported the goods, absorbed the freight and customs risk *for you*, and stand behind what they sell because they have a real business — and a real bank account — right here in Nigeria.

### "Temu is basically free."
Cheap only stays cheap if the item actually works and actually arrives as described — and Temu's biggest complaint pattern, globally, is exactly that gamble. There's also no real local accountability: if the item is wrong or broken, you're negotiating with an anonymous overseas seller through a support widget. On ChinaInNigeria, the same category of low-cost Chinese-sourced goods comes from a vendor who has passed identity verification and knows that a bad pattern of complaints gets a product taken down — or the vendor suspended.

### "Jumia & Konga already does fast delivery and buyer protection."
It does — for products already sitting in Jumia & Konga's own ecosystem. What it doesn't give you is direct access to the pricing and product range you'd get sourcing from China, because Jumia & Konga's sellers are marking up whatever they already imported through their own channels. ChinaInNigeria vendors *are* the import channel — you're buying closer to source, from someone who specializes in Chinese sourcing as their whole business, at Jumia & Konga-equivalent delivery speed.
